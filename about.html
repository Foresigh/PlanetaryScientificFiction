Planetary Science Fiction Author

Robert Pummer writes planetary science fiction — a genre he has built around a single, enormous premise: what if humanity didn't escape Earth, but moved it? The Earth Mover Universe is the result of that question taken to its fullest possible consequence.

His writing focuses on scale, consequence, and the mysteries that sit just beyond accepted boundaries. He is drawn to worlds under pressure, humans under strain, and the convergence of science, power, survival, and belief — the forces that define civilization when civilization itself is tested at the deepest level.

The Earth Mover Universe does not treat science and faith as opposites, or technology and humanity as separate concerns. The series is built on the belief that the most interesting questions arise exactly where these things collide — under conditions no one anticipated and no institution was designed to handle.

The stories explore endurance and power, wonder and consequence — what it means to carry an entire civilization, its history, its beauty, its conflict, into the deep. Not as refugees. Not as colonists. As a world that refused to be left behind.

The Vision

"Stories where the stakes extend beyond cities, nations, or even Earth itself — measured world by world."

Planetary science fiction is a natural evolution of the genre — taking the scale that the best science fiction has always gestured toward and making it literal. The planet is not the setting. The planet is the protagonist, the vessel, the thing at risk. Everything follows from that.
